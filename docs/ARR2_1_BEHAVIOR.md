## Behavioral Domains

### 1. Revelation Triggers

`Revelation` may occur only when a lawful relationship between `Traveler`
and existing `World` truth changes.

Constitutional trigger categories may include:

- travel
- observation
- interaction
- explicit inquiry
- elapsed presence

These are constitutional categories, not implementation mechanisms.

A trigger does not create truth.

It only creates a lawful condition under which existing truth may become
knowable.

### 2. Revelation Progression

`Revelation` is not required to be binary.

Knowability may unfold:

- incrementally
- sequentially
- in layers
- by dependency

One revelation may lawfully make further revelation possible.

However, progression may never:

- invent new truth
- replace existing truth
- redefine world semantics

Progression changes only how much of existing truth is knowable to a
`Traveler`.

### 3. Revelation Persistence

Revealed knowledge is not constitutionally required to be either permanent
or temporary in all cases.

Persistence is governed by constitutional rules of revelation rather than by
world truth itself.

Therefore:

- some knowledge may remain knowable once revealed
- some knowledge may remain deferred until lawful conditions are met again
- some knowledge may be stable across time
- some knowledge may depend on continued relationship with the world

Persistence governs knowability.

It does not govern existence.

### 4. Rediscovery

`Rediscovery` is constitutionally distinct from first revelation.

The same unchanged world truth may become knowable to a `Traveler` more than
once under different conditions.

Therefore:

- first revelation and rediscovery are not identical states
- rediscovery does not imply that truth ceased to exist
- rediscovery does not imply that revelation failed

`Rediscovery` governs renewed knowability of existing truth.

It does not recreate truth.

### 5. Agency

`Revelation` is relational.

Therefore its behavior may involve:

- the `World` making truth available
- the `Traveler` becoming capable of knowing it
- a lawful combination of both

`Revelation` need not be purely passive or purely active in all cases.

The constitution permits:

- revelation initiated by changed world relationship
- revelation initiated by changed traveler relationship
- revelation requiring both

What matters is not who "caused" revelation first, but whether the
relationship between `Traveler` and `World` lawfully changed such that truth
became knowable.

### 6. Behavioral Invariants

The following remain true in every lawful revelation behavior:

#### Invariant 1

`Revelation` never creates `World` truth.

#### Invariant 2

`Revelation` never modifies `World` truth.

#### Invariant 3

`Revelation` never bypasses `World` authority.

#### Invariant 4

Different `Travelers` may lawfully experience different revelation states
over the same unchanged `World`.

#### Invariant 5

`Revelation` may govern only knowability, never existence.

## Constitutional Behavior Summary

`Revelation` behaves as the lawful evolution of knowability between
`Traveler` and existing `World` truth.

It may be:

- triggered by changed relationship
- progressive rather than binary
- persistent or conditional by rule
- rediscovered without recreating truth
- relational in agency

At all times it remains subordinate to the truths established in
`ARR2_1_TRUTH.md`.
