# Graphic Empty World Expression

## Purpose

This document defines the first Graphic experience before the creator creates anything.

It does not define:

- UI implementation
- shell composition
- tool exposure
- artboard behavior
- runtime behavior

Its purpose is to answer one question:

`What should a Graphic creator experience before they create anything?`

## Governing Rule

The empty world is not a screen before the editor.

It is the first act of the creative process.

Graphic should not begin by asking the creator to understand:

- artboards
- layers
- objects
- properties
- effects
- inspector terminology
- tools

Graphic should first help the creator feel:

- I am here to communicate something visually
- I can begin without knowing every design decision yet
- I can start from a meaningful direction
- I am not trapped by the first choice I make

## First Psychological State

Before Dropple says anything, the creator is likely not thinking:

- I need an artboard
- I need a vector tool
- I need to configure a canvas

The creator is more likely thinking:

- I need to make something
- I need to communicate an idea
- I am not sure where to begin
- I do not want to choose the wrong direction

This emotional state should govern the empty world.

The empty world should reduce:

- uncertainty
- fear of starting wrong
- tool anxiety
- premature technical decisions

## What Dropple Should Immediately Communicate

Within the first few seconds, the creator should understand:

- you are here to create visual communication
- you do not need to know every design decision yet
- you can begin from a meaningful communication direction
- your work can evolve as your thinking evolves

The empty world should communicate orientation before control.

It should answer:

`You are not entering a generic graphics editor.`

It should instead communicate:

`You are beginning visual communication work.`

## First Safe Move

The first move should not be the most powerful move.

It should be the safest useful move.

For Graphic, the safest useful first move is likely:

`Choose the kind of communication you want to create.`

Examples include:

- Poster
- Social Graphic
- Brand Board
- Logo Sheet
- Flyer
- Presentation Cover

These are not templates.

They are communication directions.

The empty world should help the creator begin from intention rather than from a tool or a primitive surface.

## Empty World Promise

The empty world should make an implicit promise:

- start simply
- change direction later
- grow naturally
- nothing here traps you

This matters because Graphic should reduce fear of commitment at the beginning.

The creator should not feel that choosing an opening direction locks them into a rigid workflow.

Dropple should communicate:

- begin with a direction
- learn through creation
- refine as the composition becomes clearer

This is the Graphic expression of progressive revelation.

## Transition to Composition

Only after the creator chooses a communication direction should Dropple introduce `Composition`.

The system should not begin by teaching Composition as a technical concept.

It should introduce Composition as the natural answer to the creator's communication need.

The transition should feel like:

`You want to communicate something visually.`

`Let's begin a Composition for that work.`

This preserves the Graphic mental model already established elsewhere:

`Communication -> Composition -> Artboard -> Objects`

Composition remains the owner.

But Composition should be introduced only when the creator has enough context to understand why it exists.

## What Should Not Appear Yet

The empty world should not introduce:

- Artboard
- Layers
- Objects
- Properties
- Effects
- Vector tools
- Typography controls
- Inspector terminology

Those belong to later stages of the Graphic journey.

The empty world should remain in Product Expression territory.

Its job is not to teach the whole language.

Its job is to provide the first meaningful orientation and the first safe move.

## Success Criteria

When leaving the Graphic empty world, the creator should feel:

- I know what I am trying to communicate.
- I know the direction I am starting from.
- I was not forced into technical decisions too early.
- I feel confident taking the next step.

The empty world succeeds when the creator no longer asks:

`What am I supposed to do?`

And instead feels:

`I know what I want to communicate. Let's begin.`

## Product Consequence

If this empty world succeeds, the next step will not feel like:

`I need an artboard.`

It will feel more like:

`I have a composition in mind. Now I need somewhere for its first expression to live.`

That transition should lead naturally into first-artboard expression without collapsing Graphic back into a canvas-first editor model.
