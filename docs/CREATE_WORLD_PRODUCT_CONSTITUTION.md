# Create World Product Constitution

## Purpose

This document records the product laws governing `Create World`.

It does not define:

- runtime contracts
- reducer behavior
- canvas implementation
- shell composition details
- transition slice implementation

It exists to freeze the product truths that should remain stable while future
creative languages, transitions, and projections are implemented.

## Law 1 — One Living World

The world is universal.

Creative languages do not replace the world.

A creator should never feel that they have left Dropple and entered a different
application family simply because the language changed.

## Law 2 — Creative Languages Change Meaning, Not Existence

Creative languages redefine meaning inside the world.

They change:

- grammar
- projection
- artifact interpretation
- dominant interactions
- creator guidance

They do not change the existence of the world itself.

## Law 3 — Shared Foundation

The following remain universal across `Create World`:

- runtime truth
- world substrate
- interaction authority
- history
- memory
- navigation
- world continuity

These are the shared foundations that make Dropple one product rather than a
collection of disconnected editors.

## Law 4 — Primary Spatial Surface

The canvas is the world's primary spatial surface.

Creative languages may reinterpret what the creator sees and does on that
surface.

They may not turn the existence of the world into a language-owned editor fork.

## Law 5 — Dominant Dimensions

Some creative languages introduce an additional dominant dimension.

Examples:

| Creative Language | Dominant Dimension |
| --- | --- |
| UIUX | Spatial |
| Graphic | Spatial |
| Document | Spatial |
| Motion | Time |
| Audio | Sequence |
| Logic | Flow |

These dimensions do not create separate products.

They are different grammars expressed inside one living world.

## Law 6 — Universal World, Variable Grammar

Dropple should be understood as:

`One World`

`-> Many Languages`

`-> Many Grammars`

`-> One Creator`

The creator learns the world once.
What changes is how meaning is expressed inside it.

## Law 7 — Creative Initiation

The creator does not begin by choosing an app or an editor.

The creator begins by choosing a creative language.

Stage 1 should therefore answer:

`How do you want to begin creating today?`

Not:

- which app?
- which file?
- which editor?
- which runtime package?

## Law 8 — Creative Initiation Progression

Creative Initiation progresses through four creator realizations:

1. `Creative Intent`
2. `Creative Language`
3. `Starting Method`
4. `Begin`

This is the threshold before `Creative Arrival`.

## Law 9 — Starting Method Follows Language

Blueprints and Templates do not lead the creator journey.

They become relevant only after the creative language is known.

Once the creator has chosen a language, Dropple may derive:

- grammar
- starting method
- blueprint or template path
- first expression
- emergence path

The creator should not need to understand the architecture behind those
decisions.

## Law 10 — Blueprints and Templates Complement, Not Compete

Within `Create World`, Blueprints and Templates solve different creator
problems.

- blueprint-oriented paths shape systems, structures, and project bootstrap
- template-oriented paths instantiate expressions or expression environments

They should remain complementary.
They should not be collapsed into one generic beginning model.

## Law 11 — Discovery Before Configuration

The creator should experience meaning before they experience configuration.

This applies to:

- language choice
- first expression
- relationship revelation
- project emergence

At no point should Create World begin by asking the creator to manage software
before they understand what they are making.

## Law 12 — Threshold Before World

Creative Initiation ends at `Begin`.

`Creative Arrival` starts only after commitment.

This boundary must remain clear:

- before `Begin`, Dropple is helping the creator choose a beginning
- after `Begin`, Dropple is welcoming the creator into the living world

---

## Consequence for Product Planning

Future Create World work should be evaluated against this constitution by
asking:

- does this preserve one living world?
- does this vary language without replacing the world?
- does this keep shared foundation universal?
- does this help the creator choose a language rather than an app?
- does this delay blueprint/template mechanics until after language is known?

If the answer to any of these is `no`, the proposal requires constitutional
review before implementation.

## Conclusion

Create World is not a family of separate editors.

It is one living world in which creative languages change meaning, grammar, and
experience without replacing the world's existence.

That is the product law future Create World planning should preserve.
