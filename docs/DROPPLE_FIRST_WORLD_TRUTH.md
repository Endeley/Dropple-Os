# Dropple First World Truth

## Purpose

This document freezes the product identity of Dropple's `First World`.

It does not define:

- UI layout
- route changes
- implementation details
- runtime behavior
- canvas behavior
- blueprint mechanics
- template mechanics
- workspace mechanics

It exists so later planning and implementation can derive the identity of the
first place a creator enters, rather than guessing at it.

This document answers one question:

`What is the First World of Dropple?`

## Core Truth

The `First World` is the entrance to Dropple's Living World.

Its purpose is not to help creators create yet.

Its purpose is to help creators understand what Dropple is, what creative
languages live here, and where they belong before entering any specific
creative language.

## Product Law — Discovery Before Direction

The `First World` does not direct creators toward a predefined path.

It reveals the creative universe available within Dropple so creators can
recognize where they belong.

Discovery precedes direction.

This means the `First World` should not primarily behave like:

- a recommendation engine
- a popularity ranking
- a funnel toward one preferred workflow

Its role is to reveal possibility before asking for commitment.

## Entry Law

Every creator enters Dropple through the `First World`, regardless of platform
or device, unless they intentionally return directly to an already-existing
context through continuity.

This law protects the identity of the `First World` while preserving lawful
continuity for:

- returning creators
- shared links
- collaboration invitations
- notifications
- platform-specific resumptions

The `First World` is therefore the canonical entrance to Dropple, but not a
forced interruption of continuity.

## 1. What Is the First World?

The `First World` is the first place a creator enters through browser or any
other entry point into Dropple.

It is not:

- a homepage
- a project dashboard
- a launcher
- a marketplace page
- a blueprint gallery
- a template gallery
- an editor

It is the entrance place of the Living World.

It is where the creator first understands:

- where they are
- what kind of product Dropple is
- what kinds of creation are possible here
- which creative language may belong to them

## 2. Why Does It Exist?

The `First World` exists to answer a set of earlier questions than
`Creative Initiation`.

`Creative Initiation` asks:

`How do you want to begin creating?`

The `First World` asks something earlier:

- `What is this world?`
- `What kinds of creation live here?`
- `Which creative language feels like my place?`

It exists so creators do not have to discover Dropple by stumbling directly
into:

- project management
- marketplace browsing
- blueprint recommendations
- editor concepts

Its purpose is orientation before creation.

## Discovery Meaning

Within the `First World`, discovery means:

`the process through which a creator recognizes the Creative Language that best matches their intended form of creation.`

Discovery does not simply mean:

- search
- browsing
- filtering
- recommendation logic

It means recognition of belonging.

## 3. What Should Every Creator Immediately Understand Upon Arrival?

Upon arriving in the `First World`, every creator should immediately
understand:

1. `I am inside Dropple's Living World.`
2. `This is a place for many kinds of creation, not one editor.`
3. `Creative languages exist here, and I will eventually choose one.`
4. `I do not need to know how Dropple works yet.`
5. `This place exists to help me discover where I belong before I begin.`

The creator should not first wonder:

- `Is this a dashboard?`
- `Am I supposed to open a project?`
- `Should I browse the marketplace first?`
- `Why am I already looking at project management?`

Arrival succeeds when the creator understands the identity of the world before
being asked to act inside it.

## 4. What Belongs Here?

The following belong in the `First World`:

- orientation to Dropple as one Living World
- introduction to the existence of multiple creative languages
- language discovery
- confidence-building signals about what happens after choosing a language
- lightweight continuity into recent or existing work as a secondary surface

The dominant experience should be:

- orientation
- discovery
- belonging
- possibility

This is the place where the creator first discovers:

`There is a place for the kind of creation I want to do here.`

## 5. What Does Not Belong Here?

The following must not dominate the `First World`:

- project management
- recent projects
- continue-working prompts
- recommended blueprints
- marketplace browsing
- intent text input
- editor chrome
- runtime concepts
- language-specific authoring surfaces

These may still exist as secondary or supporting surfaces, but they must not
define the identity of the place.

The `First World` is not where the creator begins building.

It is where the creator understands where building can begin.

## 6. What Realization Tells Us the Creator Is Ready to Leave This Place?

The creator is ready to leave the `First World` when they can honestly say:

`I know which creative language or world I want to enter next.`

That is the departure condition.

At that point:

- the `First World` has done its job
- language-specific `Creative Initiation` may begin

The departure should feel like:

- entering a meaningful region of the Living World

not:

- opening an app
- launching an editor
- leaving the product for another tool

## Consequence for Later Planning

Future planning for the `First World` must preserve these truths:

1. The `First World` precedes language-specific `Creative Initiation`.
2. It helps the creator discover where they belong, not what to build yet.
3. It must be dominated by world identity and language discovery, not project
   management or marketplace behavior.
4. It must preserve continuity into the Living World and into the chosen
   creative language.
5. Discovery must precede direction.
6. The `First World` introduces possibility; creation begins only after a
   Creative Language has been chosen.

## Conclusion

The `First World` is not Dropple's homepage in the conventional sense.

It is the entrance to the Living World.

Its role is not to help the creator create yet.

Its role is to help the creator understand what Dropple is, what kinds of
creation live here, and which creative language they should enter next.

The `First World` introduces possibility; creation begins only after a
Creative Language has been chosen.
