# Dropple First World Plan

## Purpose

This document plans the behavior of the `First World` as an experience.

It does not prescribe:

- screens
- layouts
- components
- animations
- route changes
- implementation details

Those are downstream expressions of the experience defined here.

- [DROPPLE_FIRST_WORLD_REVIEW.md](/Users/endeleykonboye/Desktop/dropple-os/dropple/docs/DROPPLE_FIRST_WORLD_REVIEW.md:1)

This plan exists to answer one question:

`How should creators experience the First World before entering a Creative Language?`

## Frozen Truth

The `First World` is the entrance to Dropple's Living World.

Its purpose is orientation before creation.

It exists to help creators understand what Dropple is, what creative
languages live here, and where they belong before entering any specific
creative language.

Everything in this plan must support that truth.

## Planning Rule

This plan may derive behavior from frozen product truth.

It may not invent new truth about:

- what the `First World` is
- what `Creative Initiation` is
- which Creative Language should be preferred
- how a specific UI should render
- which implementation surface should own each behavior

## Truth, Experience, Presentation

This plan deliberately separates three layers:

1. `Truth`
   What the `First World` is.
2. `Experience`
   How creators move through the `First World`.
3. `Presentation`
   How that experience is later expressed visually and interactively.

This document defines `Experience`.

It does not yet define `Presentation`.

## Experience Responsibility

The `First World` experience must do five things well:

1. establish that the creator has entered Dropple
2. orient the creator to what kind of world this is
3. reveal the available Creative Languages without prescribing one
4. help the creator recognize where they belong
5. transfer the creator into a chosen Creative Language with confidence

## Experience States

The `First World` supports a set of experience states.

These are not rigid navigation steps.

Different creators may move through them differently depending on:

- continuity
- familiarity
- returning context
- existing intent

For example:

- a brand-new creator may move through all states
- a returning creator may arrive already near `Recognition`
- a continuity-driven entry may arrive with `Commitment` already established

The `First World` must therefore support these states without collapsing into
a fixed wizard.

## 1. Arrival

### Purpose

Establish that the creator has entered Dropple's Living World.

### What This State Must Achieve

The creator should immediately understand:

- `I am inside Dropple.`
- `This is a distinct place with a clear purpose.`
- `I have not yet entered a specific Creative Language.`

### What Must Not Happen Here

Arrival must not immediately feel like:

- a project dashboard
- a language-specific editor
- a marketplace
- a setup wizard

## 2. Orientation

### Purpose

Help the creator understand what Dropple is.

### What This State Must Achieve

The creator should understand:

- Dropple is one Living World
- many kinds of creation live here
- they are not yet being asked to build
- this place exists to help them understand where they belong

### What Must Not Happen Here

Orientation must not require the creator to first understand:

- runtime concepts
- project structure
- blueprints
- templates
- workspace mechanics

## 3. Discovery

### Purpose

Reveal the available Creative Languages without directing the creator toward
one.

### What This State Must Achieve

The creator should understand:

- multiple Creative Languages exist here
- each language represents a different way of creating
- Dropple is broader than one tool or editor

Discovery must preserve the frozen law:

`Discovery precedes direction.`

### What Must Not Happen Here

Discovery must not collapse into:

- recommendation dominance
- popularity ranking
- forced funnel behavior
- marketplace-first browsing

## 4. Recognition

### Purpose

Enable the creator to recognize the Creative Language that matches their
intended form of creation.

### What This State Must Achieve

The creator should naturally reach a realization such as:

- `This is the language that fits what I want to create.`
- `I know where I belong next.`

Recognition is not the same as search completion.

It is the moment of belonging.

### What Must Not Happen Here

Recognition must not feel like:

- software selection
- app comparison
- technical configuration
- choosing an implementation surface

## 5. Commitment

### Purpose

Confirm the creator's chosen Creative Language and prepare the transition.

### What This State Must Achieve

The creator should understand:

- they have chosen a meaningful direction
- Dropple understands where they are going next
- the world is ready to continue that journey

Commitment should feel like:

- readiness
- confidence
- threshold crossing

not:

- launching an app
- opening a file
- finishing onboarding

## 6. Exit

### Purpose

Transfer the creator into that language's `Creative Initiation`.

### What This State Must Achieve

The departure condition remains:

`I know which creative language or world I want to enter next.`

At that moment, the `First World` has completed its work and the chosen
language's `Creative Initiation` may begin.

The transfer should feel like:

- entering a meaningful region of the same Living World

not:

- leaving Dropple
- opening another product
- switching into a disconnected application

## Continuity Behavior

Because the `First World` is the canonical entrance but not a forced
interruption of continuity, this experience must remain adaptive.

That means:

- brand-new creators may need full orientation and discovery
- returning creators may move quickly into recognition
- continuity-driven entries may preserve an already-understood destination

The experience should therefore support different starting points without
losing the identity of the place itself.

## Relationship to Creative Initiation

The `First World` and `Creative Initiation` answer different questions.

`First World` asks:

- `What is Dropple?`
- `What kinds of creation live here?`
- `Which Creative Language is my place?`

`Creative Initiation` asks:

- `How do I want to begin inside this Creative Language?`

The `First World` must not take over the responsibility of
`Creative Initiation`.

It does not help the creator begin creating yet.

It helps the creator discover where creation should continue.

## What Must Dominate the Experience

The dominant experience of the `First World` should be:

- orientation
- discovery
- possibility
- belonging
- confidence about what comes next

## What Must Recede

The following may exist only as secondary or supporting surfaces:

- project continuity
- recent work
- recommended blueprints
- marketplace presence
- language-specific tools
- editor concepts

They must never dominate the identity of the place.

## Future Planning Questions

Later planning and implementation should derive from these questions:

1. How should the `First World` establish its identity immediately?
2. How should it reveal Creative Languages without prescribing one?
3. How should it support both new and returning creators without becoming a
   rigid flow?
4. How should it make recognition of belonging natural?
5. How should it prepare the threshold into language-specific
   `Creative Initiation`?

These are experience questions, not presentation decisions.
