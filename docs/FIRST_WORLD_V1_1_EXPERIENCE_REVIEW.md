# FIRST WORLD V1.1 EXPERIENCE REVIEW

## Status

`FROZEN EXPERIENCE EVIDENCE`

## Version

`V1.1`

## Date

`July 28, 2026`

## Runtime

`First World Spatial Runtime`

## Reviewer

`Codex + Creator Review Baseline`

## Purpose

Capture the first formal experience review of the `First World` after the
introduction of the `Spatial Runtime`.

This document is historical evidence.

It exists to answer:

`What did V1.1 actually feel like before V1.2 refinement began?`

It does not introduce new architecture.

It does not redesign the world.

## Review Method

The review was conducted through the seven frozen experience gates:

1. `Arrival`
2. `Orientation`
3. `Curiosity`
4. `Presence`
5. `Geography`
6. `Continuity`
7. `Invitation`

## Gate Results

| Gate | Result | Review Question | Evidence Summary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Arrival` | `PASS` | Does entering `Dropple` feel different from opening an application? | The scene reads as a place rather than a dashboard. The world is visible immediately and the browser recedes. |
| `Orientation` | `PASS` | Can I understand where I am without instructions? | The `Origin` is readable and the `World Core` is clearly the central landmark. |
| `Curiosity` | `REFINE` | Do I want to explore? | The anchors are visible, but they still read more like markers than destinations. |
| `Presence` | `REFINE` | Does the world exist without me? | The world has mood, but still feels close to a staged scene rather than a continuously living place. |
| `Geography` | `REFINE` | Can I form a mental map? | Objects are visible in space, but relationships between places, distances, and routes are still unclear. |
| `Continuity` | `PASS` | Do I ever feel like I left `Dropple`? | Nothing in the current view suggests page switching or application rupture. |
| `Invitation` | `REFINE` | Does the world invite creation? | The world suggests possibility, but does not yet clearly indicate the first meaningful destination or beginning of creation. |

## Evidence Log

| Gate | Observation | Severity | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Curiosity` | Anchors feel like floating markers more than places. | `High` | They are visible and distinct, but they do not yet read as districts with character. |
| `Geography` | Distances and routes between `Origin` and the surrounding regions are unclear. | `High` | The creator can see objects in space, but cannot easily form a mental map of where places are relative to one another. |
| `Presence` | Ambient life is present, but still too thin to make the world feel self-sustaining. | `Medium` | The mood is established, but the world still feels staged rather than continuously alive. |

## Verdict

`REFINE`

## Motion Evidence Addendum

Review evidence was later strengthened by screen-recording observation rather
than still imagery alone.

That motion evidence changed the assessment in three important ways:

### 1. Spatial Runtime Confidence Increased

The `Spatial Runtime` no longer reads as a visual experiment.

Camera movement, parallax, and depth collectively confirm that the `First
World` is behaving like the beginning of a genuine world runtime rather than
a static 3D demo.

### 2. Presence Improved

Subtle motion materially improved the `Presence` gate.

The world now feels inhabited rather than frozen.

This evidence supports a revised runtime conclusion:

- `Arrival` remains `PASS`
- `Orientation` remains `PASS`
- `Presence` improves to `PASS`
- `Continuity` remains `PASS`

### 3. Composition Became The Primary Bottleneck

The limiting factor is no longer rendering technology.

The limiting factor is world composition.

The strongest unresolved issue observed in motion is this:

`The world ends visually about 15–20 meters from Origin.`

In practice, the creator perceives:

- several objects around `Origin`

instead of:

- a larger world continuing beyond `Origin`

This means the unresolved refinement burden concentrates on:

- `Curiosity`
- `Geography`
- `Invitation`

## Revised Gate Assessment From Motion Evidence

| Gate | Result | Motion-Based Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Arrival` | `PASS` | Entering Dropple feels distinct from opening an application. |
| `Orientation` | `PASS` | The `Origin` and `World Core` remain readable during movement. |
| `Presence` | `PASS` | Subtle motion and parallax make the world feel inhabited. |
| `Continuity` | `PASS` | Motion does not rupture the illusion of one continuous place. |
| `Curiosity` | `REFINE` | Anchors are more interesting in motion, but still read more like markers than destinations. |
| `Geography` | `REFINE` | Objects are positioned in space, but they do not yet define space. |
| `Invitation` | `REFINE` | The world does not yet clearly communicate where the first meaningful journey should begin. |

## Summary Assessment

The `Spatial Runtime` has proven itself.

The architecture is no longer the limiting factor.

The next challenge is not rendering technology.

The next challenge is world experience:

- turning anchors into destinations
- making geography legible
- making routes perceivable
- making the first invitation to create feel natural

## Frozen Conclusion

`V1.1` successfully proves that the First World can be rendered as a genuine
spatial place without breaking Dropple's architectural boundaries.

`V1.1` does not yet prove that the world is fully believable as a place to
inhabit and navigate.

That evidence becomes the baseline against which `V1.2` must be judged.
