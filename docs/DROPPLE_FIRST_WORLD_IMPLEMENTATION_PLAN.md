## 1. Structural Translation

### Structural Responsibility

The `First World` must be realized as a place with clear internal structure.

That structure must answer:

- what persistent regions exist
- what temporary regions exist
- what relationships those regions have
- what transitions the creator may move through

without prescribing visual layout.

### Primary Structural Regions

The following structural regions must exist in the `First World`:

1. `World Identity Region`
   Establishes where the creator is and what kind of world this is.

2. `Creative Language Discovery Region`
   Reveals the available Creative Languages as meaningful possibilities within
   Dropple.

3. `Recognition Region`
   Supports the moment when the creator recognizes which language belongs to
   their intended form of creation.

4. `Continuity Support Region`
   Supports lawful continuity into recent, existing, or returning context
   without dominating the identity of the place.

5. `Transition Region`
   Prepares and confirms movement from the `First World` into a chosen
   language's `Creative Initiation`.

These are structural regions, not UI components.

### Structural Hierarchy

The structural hierarchy of the `First World` must be:

1. world identity
2. language discovery
3. recognition and belonging
4. continuity as secondary support
5. transition into chosen language

This hierarchy prevents the place from collapsing into:

- a dashboard
- a launcher
- a project browser
- a marketplace-first surface

### Persistent vs Temporary Structure

The following should be structurally persistent:

- the identity of Dropple as one Living World
- the existence of multiple Creative Languages
- the possibility of belonging within one of them

The following may be structurally temporary or adaptive:

- continuity emphasis for returning creators
- recognition emphasis once intent is already known
- transition emphasis once a language has been chosen

Temporary structure may adapt to creator context.

It may not redefine the identity of the place.

### Structural Relationships

The `First World` must preserve these relationships:

- `Dropple` contains many Creative Languages
- the creator stands before language-specific creation
- continuity supports orientation but does not replace it
- language choice precedes `Creative Initiation`
- `Creative Initiation` precedes creation inside a chosen language

## 2. Experience Translation

The structural regions above must support the frozen experience states.

These states are not rigid steps.

They are creator states that the structure must lawfully support.

### Arrival

Structural responsibility:

- foreground the `World Identity Region`
- suppress any ambiguity that the creator has entered a generic dashboard or
  editor

### Orientation

Structural responsibility:

- keep the `World Identity Region` active
- reveal that Dropple contains many forms of creation

### Discovery

Structural responsibility:

- foreground the `Creative Language Discovery Region`
- ensure that possibilities are revealed without prescription

### Recognition

Structural responsibility:

- allow the `Recognition Region` to become more prominent as belonging becomes
  clearer
- support self-recognition rather than software selection

### Commitment

Structural responsibility:

- activate the `Transition Region`
- make the threshold into a chosen language feel confirmed and meaningful

### Exit

Structural responsibility:

- hand the creator into language-specific `Creative Initiation`
- preserve world continuity during that transition

### Continuity Adaptation

For returning or continuity-driven creators:

- the `Continuity Support Region` may become more relevant earlier
- the experience may enter near `Recognition` or `Commitment`

Even then, the structure must still preserve the identity of the `First World`
as orientation before creation.

## 3. Presentation Constraints

Presentation remains free to evolve visually, but it must preserve the
following constraints.

### Constraint 1: Identity Before Utility

Presentation must first communicate:

- where the creator is
- what kind of world this is
- that multiple Creative Languages exist here

It must not first communicate:

- project management
- tool access
- editor utility

### Constraint 2: Discovery Before Direction

Presentation must reveal possibilities without implying that Dropple has
already chosen the right path for the creator.

This protects the `Discovery Before Direction` law.

### Constraint 3: Belonging Before Configuration

Presentation must support the creator recognizing:

`This is where I belong next.`

before asking for setup, configuration, or workflow decisions.

### Constraint 4: Continuity Without Dominance

Presentation may acknowledge:

- recent work
- ongoing work
- resumable context

But these must remain supportive, not identity-defining.

### Constraint 5: Same World, Different Threshold

Presentation must preserve the feeling that the creator remains in one Living
World while approaching a meaningful threshold into a specific Creative
Language.

It must not feel like:

- leaving Dropple
- opening another application
- launching an editor

## 4. Implementation Boundaries

Implementation may choose:

- concrete components
- layout patterns
- navigation patterns
- interaction affordances
- adaptive presentation for new and returning creators

Implementation may not alter:

- the identity of the `First World`
- the departure condition of the place
- the separation between `First World` and `Creative Initiation`
- the dominance hierarchy of orientation over project management
- the law that Creative Language precedes language-specific creation

### Implementation Guardrails

Implementation must not allow the `First World` to be primarily defined by:

- recent projects
- blueprint recommendations
- marketplace browsing
- intent text entry
- editor chrome
- runtime concepts

These may appear only as structurally subordinate expressions.

### Transition Guardrail

Implementation must ensure that leaving the `First World` means:

the creator is entering a chosen Creative Language

not:

the creator is entering creation in general.

That distinction is constitutionally important because
`Creative Initiation` still owns the question:

`How do I want to begin inside this language?`

## Out of Scope

This document does not define:

- final screen designs
- exact copy
- final interaction flows
- animation systems
- route mechanics
- runtime ownership
- workspace implementation

Those belong to later implementation and validation work.

## Success Condition

This implementation plan is complete when future work can answer:

`How should the First World be structurally realized so presentation and implementation can express it without changing its truth?`

At that point, product implementation of the `First World` may begin.
