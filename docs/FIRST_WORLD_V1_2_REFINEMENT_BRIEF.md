## Mission

Transform anchors into destinations.

This is the single governing objective of `V1.2`.

Every refinement must strengthen the creator's mental map of the world.

`V1.2` is not a polish pass.

`V1.1` proved the world can exist.

`V1.2` must prove the world can be understood.

Current mental model:

`Origin`

`-> Marker`

`-> Another Marker`

Target mental model:

`Origin`

`-> Journey`

`-> District`

`-> Creation`

The purpose of `V1.2` is to move the creator through this progression:

`I can see somewhere.`

`-> I know what that place is.`

`-> I know how to get there.`

`-> I want to go there.`

The refined world-design sequence is:

`Create geography.`

`-> Use geography to hide and reveal.`

`-> Let revealed places become districts.`

`-> Let districts become destinations.`

The `First World` should now be designed mentally as a continent, not a
plaza.

This is a scale-of-thinking correction, not a lore change.

The goal is not to surround `Origin` with options.

The goal is to connect `Origin` to journeys.

## Foundational Layout Correction

The current world must stop behaving like a radial menu.

`Origin` is the beginning of the world, not the center of an orbit of equally
visible destinations.

`Origin` should be connected, not surrounded.

The world should not read like:

`Origin`

`-> ring of visible districts`

The world should begin to read like:

`Origin`

`-> route`

`-> intermediate geography`

`-> district`

`-> farther world beyond`

This is the central layout correction for `V1.2`.

## Scope Reduction

`V1.2` should not attempt to fully rebuild every district.

`V1.2` should prove the world model with one exemplary journey from `Origin`
to one first district.

That journey should be strong enough to teach:

- how journeys begin
- how routes are read
- how geography reveals destinations
- how districts are approached
- how arrival differs from selection

If that one journey works, later districts become expansion rather than
invention.

## Deliverables

### 1. Legible Geography

The creator should be able to understand:

- what is close
- what is far
- how regions relate
- where the world extends

The world should begin to form a mental map.

This includes a mental horizon.

The world must feel larger than what is immediately visible.

The creator should not feel that the world ends a short distance from
`Origin`.

Standing at `Origin`, the creator should not clearly see every major
destination at once.

Some places should be:

- nearby
- distant
- partially hidden
- only hinted at
- only discoverable through travel

Not every district deserves equal visibility from `Origin`.

### 2. Hide And Reveal

The world should not expose every important place at once.

Do not spread every district evenly around `Origin` in a way that makes the
world read like a spatial menu.

Discovery should be supported through partial occlusion and staged reveal.

Examples:

- a ridge partially hides a district
- a bridge reveals the next area
- a gateway frames a destination
- a curve in the terrain delays the reveal

The purpose is not to hide content arbitrarily.

The purpose is to create anticipation, discovery, and memory.

This is now a world law for `V1.2`:

`The world must never reveal itself all at once. Every journey should reveal more than the last.`

## Great Route Principle

The world should contain a primary route leaving `Origin`.

This does not need to be literal asphalt.

It may be:

- a road
- a bridge sequence
- a ridgeline path
- a light corridor
- a geographic passage

Its purpose is to communicate:

`This is how journeys begin.`

The first journey should not feel like choosing from equal options.

It should feel like entering the world's main path and discovering what lies
beyond it.

## Principle Of Commitment

When a creator leaves `Origin`, they should feel like they have started
something.

This does not mean they are trapped.

It means the world has gently asked them to commit to a direction.

As a journey progresses:

- `Origin` should become less visually dominant
- the route should become more legible than the place being left behind
- the creator should eventually feel:
  `I am somewhere else now.`

If `Origin` remains equally visible from everywhere, the world will not feel
large.

### 3. District Identity

Each anchor must stop reading as a colored object in space.

Each anchor must begin reading as the visible entrance to a distinct place.

Examples of evidence:

- surrounding structural silhouettes
- district-specific spatial character
- readable shape language
- recognizable environmental identity

The purpose is not to reveal the editor.

The purpose is to suggest the nature of the place before arrival.

### 4. Visible Routes

The world must imply movement between places.

This may be expressed through:

- paths
- terrain contours
- bridges
- light channels
- elevated passages
- energy flows

The specific form is flexible.

The requirement is not.

Places must feel connected.

### 5. First Destination

One destination should become the natural first place to approach.

This should not feel forced.

It should feel like the most readable next step in the world.

Secondary and distant destinations may remain visible, but the world should
establish hierarchy rather than equal attraction.

## Implementation Law

No visual element may exist solely because it looks good.

Every visual element must improve at least one of the following:

- orientation
- memory
- destination identity

If an element does not strengthen understanding, it does not belong in
`V1.2`.

When evaluating any addition, ask:

`If this disappeared, would the creator's mental map become weaker?`

If the answer is `no`, the element likely does not belong in this iteration.

Examples:

Good:

- a ridge that separates one district from another
- a hill that hides part of a future district until approach
- a horizon line that suggests additional world beyond the immediately visible basin
- a road or corridor that reveals the next place in sequence
- a bridge, arch, gateway, or threshold that makes approach memorable
- a route that clarifies where the first journey leads
- a beacon that makes a destination legible from afar

Bad:

- random floating props with no spatial meaning
- decorative effects that do not improve readability
- spectacle that weakens the mental map
- districts arranged evenly around `Origin` like menu options

## World Vocabulary

`V1.2` should be implemented using world vocabulary rather than generic scene
vocabulary.

Do not primarily think in terms of:

- prop
- decoration
- filler object

Think in terms of:

- ridge
- valley
- hill
- highway
- bridge
- overlook
- plaza
- gateway
- threshold
- district
- path
- route
- horizon
- beacon

The question is not:

`What object should be added?`

The question is:

`What geographical feature does this world need in order to be understood?`

## Implementation Order

`V1.2` should be implemented in this order:

### Step 1 - Legible Geography

The world must first become spatially understandable.

Standing at `Origin`, the creator should begin to understand:

- what is near
- what is far
- what the broad layout of the world is
- that more exists beyond immediate sight

The creator should begin to perceive a world horizon, not merely a ring of
objects around `Origin`.

The first design question is not:

`Where do the districts go around Origin?`

It is:

`What does the creator encounter first, and what lies beyond it?`

The first route outward from `Origin` should become more important than the
full visibility of many destinations.

### Step 2 - Hide And Reveal

Do not show every major destination equally and simultaneously.

Use geography to create anticipation.

The world should reveal itself gradually through:

- ridges
- hills
- gateways
- curves
- framed vistas

This is how curiosity becomes spatial rather than instructional.

The creator should begin to form memories such as:

- past the ridge
- beyond the bridge
- after the gateway
- over the rise

That is stronger than remembering a colored marker floating at one side of
`Origin`.

### Step 2.5 - Approach Design

Before a district is treated as a final destination, its approach must exist.

Every district must have an approach, not just an arrival.

Approach is where anticipation is built.

Arrival is where confirmation happens.

Without an approach, districts collapse back into menu items.

### Step 2.6 - Commitment Threshold

Each first journey should include a moment where the creator is no longer
merely looking outward from `Origin`, but has meaningfully left it behind.

This may be achieved through:

- distance
- curvature
- elevation change
- a bridge
- a gateway
- occlusion

The purpose is to create a psychological shift from:

`I am still at Origin looking at options.`

to:

`I am on my way somewhere.`

### Step 3 - District Identity

Once geography and reveal exist, districts should become recognizable from a
distance by silhouette, character, and atmosphere.

The goal is recognition, not detail.

### Step 4 - Visible Routes

Only after geography is legible should routes become explicit.

Routes should communicate relationship as much as movement.

They should silently answer:

- how places connect
- which places belong together
- how a journey might unfold

### Step 5 - First Destination

Only after geography, reveal, identity, and routes exist should the world
emphasize the first destination.

The first destination should not feel like a forced tutorial.

It should feel like the most natural place to begin.

## Explicit Non-Goals

`V1.2` must not introduce:

- new architecture
- new runtime layers
- new constitutional documents
- game mechanics
- avatars
- physics systems
- broad tooling expansion
- unrelated editor redesign

This phase refines world experience only.

## Implementation Rule

No new architecture may be introduced during this refinement pass.

When a deficiency is found, the first response must be:

- can this be solved with world composition?
- can this be solved with motion?
- can this be solved with lighting?
- can this be solved with geography?
- can this be solved with interaction?

Only if all five answers are `no` may architecture be reconsidered.

## Success Criteria

`V1.2` is ready for review when the world more clearly communicates:

- each anchor is becoming a destination
- destinations belong to a shared geography
- geography creates reveal rather than full immediate exposure
- the world extends mentally beyond the immediately visible area
- the first route is perceivable
- one first destination naturally invites approach
- the creator can begin forming a mental map of where places are
- the visible world no longer reads like equally spaced options around `Origin`
- one complete journey from `Origin` to a first district can teach the world model on its own

## Definition Of Done

`V1.2` is complete only if all of the following are true:

### 1. District Identity

Every district is recognizable by:

- form
- composition
- atmosphere

even if its label is hidden.

### 2. Legible Geography

Standing at `Origin`, a creator can infer:

- the approximate layout of the world
- the broad relationship between districts

without requiring explanation.

The world must not read as a small circle of visible objects surrounding the
creator.

The world must also not read as an orbit of equally available choices.

### 3. Hide And Reveal

The world does not expose every important place at once.

At least part of the surrounding world is discovered through composition,
occlusion, or approach.

Some destinations should be beyond the immediate horizon and only become
legible through movement.

### 4. Visible Routes

Connections between `Origin` and destinations are naturally perceivable
through world composition rather than explicit instruction.

### 5. First Destination

One destination clearly communicates through the environment itself:

`This is where your journey begins.`

### 6. Approach

The first destination has a memorable approach sequence before arrival.

The creator experiences at least some combination of:

- departure from `Origin`
- route progression
- threshold crossing
- partial reveal
- final arrival

### 7. Return Legibility

After reaching the first district, the creator can return to `Origin`
without relying on interface hints alone.

The return should confirm that a mental map has formed.

`V1.2` is not complete unless the follow-up experience review confirms:

- `I can distinguish one district from another.`
- `I can predict where to travel next.`
- `I remember where I have been.`
- `The world feels larger than what is currently visible.`
- `If labels disappear, I can still identify the districts.`

## Review Requirement

After implementation, `V1.2` must be judged by re-running the same experience
review gates used for `V1.1`:

- `Arrival`
- `Orientation`
- `Curiosity`
- `Presence`
- `Geography`
- `Continuity`
- `Invitation`

In addition to the seven gates, `V1.2` must include one mental-map check.

The preferred review evidence is a short screen recording rather than a still
screenshot.

The review should observe:

- arrival at `Origin`
- looking around
- choosing a destination
- travelling there
- returning

The preferred `V1.2` review path is one complete journey:

`Origin`

`-> primary route`

`-> threshold or landmark`

`-> first district`

`-> return`

Orientation, continuity, and mental mapping must be judged through motion,
not static composition alone.

### Mental Map Check

After a short period of exploration, ask:

`Can the creator draw a rough sketch of the world's layout from memory?`

Not accurately.

Approximately.

For example:

`Media`

`Build      Origin      Design`

`Collaborate`

If a creator can roughly remember where places are, the world is becoming
cognitively coherent rather than merely visually appealing.

## Standing Review Questions

The `V1.2` review should explicitly answer:

- Can I immediately distinguish one district from another?
- Can I predict where I should travel next without being told?
- Do I remember where I have been after moving through the world?
- Does the world feel larger than what is currently visible?
- Can I sketch the world's layout from memory?
- If all labels disappeared, could I still identify the districts?
- Did the first district have an approach, not just an arrival?
- At what moment did I feel I had truly left `Origin`?
- Could I find my way back without relying on labels or UI hints?

## One-Sentence Completion Test

`V1.2` is complete when a first-time creator can travel from Origin to one
district, remember the route, return to Origin, and feel like they visited a
real place rather than switched screens.

The purpose is not to ask whether the world changed.

The purpose is to ask whether the world improved.

## Expected Outcome

`V1.2` should make the following sentence feel true:

`The First World is no longer just a spatial scene. It is becoming a navigable set of creative destinations inside one continuous world.`
