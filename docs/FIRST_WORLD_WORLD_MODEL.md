# First World World Model

## Purpose

This document lawfully reopens only the `Structure` layer of the `First World`
artifact chain.

It exists because implementation revealed that the `First World` structure is
incomplete.

The missing structural dimension is not visual.

It is world-native.

This document therefore answers one question:

`What kinds of things are allowed to exist inside the First World's governed universe?`

This model is scoped only to the `First World` at `/`.

- not a general product theory
- not a behavior plan

## Structure Reopening Statement

The `First World` already has:

- frozen truth
- frozen behavior
- frozen implementation translation

Implementation revealed that the `Structure` layer was incomplete because it
implicitly borrowed a browser-native model rather than defining a world-native
entity model.

This document addresses that incompleteness without reopening:

- identity
- behavior
- governance

## Core Law

`Relationships cannot be defined until the entities that participate in those relationships have been defined.`

This law governs the order of structural modeling in the `First World`.

Before defining:

- travel
- distance
- entry
- proximity
- continuity across regions

the participating entities must first be defined.

## Entry Law

`Entering a region does not leave the Living World. It changes the creator's relationship to that world.`

This law protects continuity.

The creator does not exit one place and enter another product.

The creator remains inside one Living World while their relationship to that
world becomes more focused, more local, and more specific.

## World-Native Entities

The `First World` may contain the following world-native entities:

- `World`
- `World Core`
- `Region`
- `Landmark`
- `Living Object`
- `Creator`
- `Camera`
- `Path`
- `Portal`
- `Atmosphere`
- `Memory`
- `Event`

No other entity type is assumed by this model.

Later artifacts may extend this set only through lawful structural review.

## 1. World

### What It Is

The `World` is the continuous environment in which the `First World` exists.

It is the containing reality of the browser root.

### Why It Exists

It exists so the `First World` can be experienced as one Living World rather
than as a sequence of isolated surfaces.

### What It Owns

The `World` owns:

- continuity
- containment of regions
- persistence of shared existence
- the existence of global world objects

### Persistence

`Persistent`

### What It Must Not Become

The `World` must not become:

- a page
- a route-local presentation trick
- a temporary wrapper around unrelated places

## 2. World Core

### What It Is

The `World Core` is the orienting origin of the `First World`.

It is the place from which discovery begins and to which the world remains
relationally anchored.

### Why It Exists

It exists so the `First World` has a meaningful origin rather than a
procedural beginning.

### What It Owns

The `World Core` owns:

- orientation origin
- the initial sense of belonging to the world
- the creator's first understanding of where they are

### Persistence

`Persistent`

### What It Must Not Become

The `World Core` must not become:

- merely the first stop in a sequence
- an isolated introduction detached from the rest of the world
- a disposable preface that disappears once another region is observed

## 3. Region

### What It Is

A `Region` is a persistent territory within the `World` that represents one
Creative Language.

### Why It Exists

It exists so each Creative Language can be encountered as part of the same
world rather than as a separate product destination.

### What It Owns

A `Region` owns:

- its own local identity
- its own landmarks
- its own local atmosphere
- its own living objects
- its own motion or behavioral signature

### Persistence

`Persistent`

### What It Must Not Become

A `Region` must not become:

- a standalone application
- an isolated surface with no world continuity
- a mere category label without territorial meaning

## 4. Landmark

### What It Is

A `Landmark` is a recognizable structural feature inside a `Region` that
helps the creator identify, navigate, or understand that region.

### Why It Exists

It exists so a region can become legible through meaningful features rather
than abstract labeling alone.

### What It Owns

A `Landmark` owns:

- local recognition
- regional orientation
- stable points of attention within a region

### Persistence

`Persistent`

### What It Must Not Become

A `Landmark` must not become:

- a generic decorative object
- a disconnected visual ornament
- a hidden implementation artifact with no world meaning

## 5. Living Object

### What It Is

A `Living Object` is a world-native object that expresses activity, presence,
meaning, or interaction inside the world.

### Why It Exists

It exists so the world can feel inhabited, reactive, and alive rather than
empty or purely informational.

### What It Owns

A `Living Object` owns:

- local presence
- local reactivity
- meaningful environmental expression

### Persistence

`Persistent` or `Transient`, depending on context

### What It Must Not Become

A `Living Object` must not become:

- a purely decorative effect with no world role
- an implementation-only UI fragment
- an abstract control detached from world meaning

### Why It Exists

It exists because the `First World` is not merely displayed.

### What It Owns

The `Creator` owns:

### Persistence

`Persistent`

`Persistent`

### What It Is

A `Path` is a lawful connective structure through which the creator may travel

### Why It Exists

It exists so movement across the world can feel meaningful rather than abrupt

### What It Owns

A `Path` owns:

### Persistence

`Persistent`

## 9. Portal

### What It Is

A `Portal` is a world-native threshold that bridges movement into a more
focused or discontinuous relationship within the world.

### Why It Exists

It exists so transitions can remain world-native even when movement is not
purely continuous.

### What It Owns

A `Portal` owns:

- threshold crossing
- explicit directional transfer
- lawful discontinuous entry when needed

### Persistence

`Transient` or `Persistent`, depending on whether the portal is enduring or
contextual

### What It Must Not Become

A `Portal` must not become:

- a generic navigation device with no world identity
- a hidden route change disguised as world behavior
- a replacement for all movement

`Persistent`

`Memory` is the persistent record of prior relationship, continuity, and

### What It Owns

`Memory` owns:

- continuity context

### Persistence

`Persistent`

## Persistent Entities

The following are persistent within the `First World`:

- `World`
- `World Core`
- `Region`
- `Landmark`
- `Creator`
- `Camera`
- `Path`
- `Atmosphere`
- `Memory`

Some `Living Objects` may also be persistent.

Persistent means they continue to exist regardless of the creator's current
focus.

## Transient Entities

The following are transient within the `First World`:

- `Event`
- some `Portal` instances
- some `Living Objects`

Transient means they appear because of context, interaction, or moment rather
than because they are permanent structural facts of the world.

## Entity Ownership

Ownership in the `First World` follows this model:

- `World` owns global continuity
- `World Core` owns orientation origin
- `Region` owns regional identity
- `Landmark` owns recognizable local structure
- `Living Object` owns local presence or activity
- `Creator` owns participation and recognition
- `Camera` owns viewpoint movement
- `Path` owns continuity of travel
- `Portal` owns threshold crossing
- `Atmosphere` owns ambient cohesion
- `Memory` owns continuity context
- `Event` owns temporary world change

No entity should silently own another entity's authority.

## Entity Lifecycle

Entity lifecycle in the `First World` should be understood as follows:

- `World` persists
- `World Core` persists
- `Regions` persist whether or not they are currently observed
- `Landmarks` persist as regional recognition structures
- `Atmosphere` persists globally, while regional atmosphere may intensify or
  recede
- `Memory` persists across entries and continuity
- `Camera` persists as viewpoint authority
- `Paths` persist as lawful connective structures
- `Portals` may persist or arise contextually
- `Events` arise, occur, and resolve
- `Living Objects` may persist or appear in response to context

This lifecycle is structural.

It does not yet define exact behavior, movement, or expression.

## World Objects Versus Regional Objects

### World Objects

The following belong to the world as a whole:

- `World`
- `World Core`
- `Camera`
- `Atmosphere`
- `Memory`
- global `Paths`
- global `Events`

These should remain meaningful regardless of the creator's current region.

### Regional Objects

The following belong primarily to regions:

- `Region`
- regional `Landmarks`
- regional `Living Objects`
- regional atmosphere intensification
- region-local `Paths`
- region-local `Portals`
- region-local `Events`

These exist inside the world but derive their meaning from a specific region.

This distinction protects continuity.

It explains why some things should span the whole world while others should
remain region-specific.

## What This Model Does Not Define

This model does not define:

- how entities are visually expressed
- how movement is rendered
- how distance is perceived
- how entry is animated
- how creator input affects the camera
- how regions are arranged relative to one another
- how landmarks become visible

Those questions belong to later structural and behavioral artifacts.

This document defines existence, not relation.

## Relationship to the Logical Model

The `Logical Model` should derive from this document.

Its responsibility is not to redefine what exists.

- what responsibilities these entities have
- what each entity is for
- what authority each entity may or may not exercise

In other words:

The `World Model` defines existence.

The `Logical Model` defines responsibility.

## Relationship to the Spatial Model

The `Spatial Model` should also derive from this document.

Its responsibility is not to define new entity types.

Its responsibility is to define:

- how entities relate in space
- how regions are arranged
- how distance changes perception
- how travel works
- how continuity is preserved while moving through the world

In other words:

The `World Model` defines what exists.

The `Spatial Model` defines how those entities may relate spatially.

## Consequences for Future First World Planning

Future `First World` planning should preserve these consequences:

1. the `First World` must be modeled as a world, not as a sequence of
   unrelated surfaces
2. `Regions` are not reducible to generic destinations
3. movement must ultimately derive from `Camera`, `Path`, `Portal`, and
   `Atmosphere`, not from document-native progression assumptions
4. continuity must remain a property of the `World`
5. entering a region must preserve belonging to the same Living World
6. future structural artifacts must derive relationships from these entities
   rather than inventing them ad hoc

## Success Condition

This model is complete when later `First World` structural work can answer:

`What kinds of things exist in the First World's governed universe?`

without borrowing document-native assumptions or reopening the frozen truth of
what the `First World` is.
